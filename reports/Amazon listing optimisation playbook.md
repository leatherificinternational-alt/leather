# Fix Leatherific's listings before Amazon's AI does

*Working playbook, as of 17 October 2026. Covers Amazon.com (US), Amazon.ca (CA) and Amazon.com.au (AU).*

Every Leatherific listing currently breaks Amazon's 75-character title rule. Titles run from 142 to 209 characters, against a cap that has applied since 27 July 2026. None of the listings uses the new Item Highlights field. Several contain emojis, a promotional claim, an environmental claim or a federation-approval claim that Amazon's rules or FTC guidance do not allow. On top of the rule breaks, the product facts contradict each other in ways that plausibly drive the returns and ratings problems now showing on the page. The spiked collar is called brass in the bullets, alloy in the attributes and glued in the A+. The CA wrist straps are listed as a belt. The mouse pad gives two different bases. The coaster description describes a different product altogether. That matters more in 2026 than before, because three surfaces now judge a listing mostly on its structured, consistent facts. The first is Amazon's assistant, Alexa for Shopping, which replaced Rufus in the US on 13 May 2026. The second is the COSMO knowledge graph. The third is a 365-day price history that shoppers can now see. Independent studies find the assistant almost never recommends products below about 4.0 stars or out of stock. That threshold puts the mouse pad (3.6) and coasters (3.9) outside it today and the belt and leash (4.0) on its edge. The work splits into three phases. This week, before the agency-recommended edit freeze around 20 October and the BFCM deal deadline the same day, Leatherific should rewrite titles and Item Highlights itself so Amazon's AI does not do it mid-peak, strip non-compliant claims, fix the wrong attributes and the broken CA strap family, and decide on deals. In November it should shift effort to stock, ads hygiene and reviews. In January it should run main-image and A+ experiments, Vine enrolments and monthly Alexa for Shopping tests. The facts only the seller can confirm (spike metal by colour, strap width and loop type, mouse pad base, leather grade and thickness) are prerequisites for most of the copy below.

## How to read the evidence labels

The playbook separates what Amazon has said from what others believe. **Amazon rule** means an Amazon help page, an Amazon staff forum post, an Amazon blog or the SP-API changelog. **Amazon, via press** means Amazon wording quoted by trade press where the original could not be read; treat it as near-confirmed. **Trade press** means reporting without a primary Amazon quote. **Agency opinion** means agency, tool-vendor or practitioner views, or their observational studies; it is directional, often commercially motivated, and never an Amazon rule. **Law** means FTC or other legal sources. **Audit** means what the 17 October 2026 live-page scrapes of Leatherific's own listings showed; these are verified observations. Where the research found conflicting claims, the conflict is stated instead of resolved.

## Trust signals: about 4.0 stars, returns and honest prices gate everything else

**Reviews and Vine.** Star ratings are a machine-learned weighting that favours recent, helpful and verified reviews ([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2015/amazon-changes-its-influential-formula-for-calculating-product-ratings/)). Recent reviews therefore move the displayed rating faster than the lifetime average suggests. Amazon's Vine page is the authority on fees. It costs nothing for up to 2 units, $75 for 3–10 units and $200 for 11–30 units per parent (CAD 100 and 240 in Canada). Billing comes only after 30 days and the first review, and nothing is charged if no review arrives within 90 days. Eligibility requires fewer than 30 reviews, FBA offers and Brand Registry. Vine runs in the US, CA and AU among others ([sell.amazon.com](https://sell.amazon.com/tools/vine)). Agency claims of "quarterly tiers" contradict that page and are wrong. Five Leatherific parents qualify on review count: straps (22), mouse pad (20), leash (12), coasters (10) and tray (4). The collar (247) and belt (47) do not. The order matters. Fix the facts and the main image first, then enrol, because Vine reviewers will flag the same contradictions shoppers do. Use the Request a Review button for everything else; incentivised or rating-steered requests are prohibited ([eComEngine](https://www.ecomengine.com/blog/amazon-review-guidelines)).

**The assistant's apparent rating floor.** This is observational, not an Amazon rule. AMALYTIX found essentially no recommended product below 4.0 stars across more than 1,300 picks, with 98.4% in stock. Mars United and Profitero found the same 4-star floor across 1,000+ picks ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/rufus-shows-5-products-not-50-what-brands-must-know-about-amazons-ai-filter/); [Mars United](https://www.marsunited.com/what-makes-rufus-tick-insights-into-amazons-agentic-search-results/)). For Leatherific, this makes rating repair on the mouse pad and coasters a precondition for any AI-visibility work on them.

**Returns badge.** The "Frequently returned item" badge became more prominent from February 2026 ([ChannelX](https://channelx.world/2026/02/amazon-frequently-returned-item-badge/)). Agencies report that it is measured against the category on a trailing window of about 3 months. They say it cannot be appealed and clears only after the rate stays below the threshold, often 4–8 weeks. They add that Amazon now shows "similar items customers keep" alternatives beneath badged listings, and they estimate a 20–40% conversion hit, which they call directional ([Velocity Sellers](https://www.velocitysellers.com/2026/09/10/amazon-frequently-returned-item-badge-creative-2026/)). Amazon publishes none of these details. The US collar carries the badge. Its listing invites "not as described" and "wrong size" returns: toy breeds listed for a 14–26 in collar, cat as a target species, rhinestones on colours that have none, inconsistent spike metal, gold versus silver spikes not explained by variant, and "adjustable width". The fix is to pull Voice of the Customer return reasons, then correct each cause in attributes, bullets and images. December gifting returns land in January, which is when badge risk peaks.

**Pricing signals.** These are confirmed through Amazon wording quoted in trade press. Since 23 April 2026, a List Price strikethrough shows only if that price was recently offered elsewhere or bought on Amazon as the Featured Offer. Since 18 May, Typical Price includes promotional sales when the product sat below its non-promotional median for more than half of 90 days ([EcomCrew](https://www.ecomcrew.com/amazon-cracks-down-on-inflated-discounts-with-new-pricing-rules-starting-april-23/)). Shoppers can see up to 365 days of the lowest daily Featured Offer price ([About Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/retail/how-to-check-amazon-price-history)). Prime Exclusive Discounts need at least 5% off the 30-day low, the current price and the reference price ([Velocity Sellers](https://www.velocitysellers.com/2026/09/28/amazon-price-history-365-days-sellers/)). The implications for Leatherific follow. The collar at $45.99, with Magenta at $48.99, sits above most competitors at $9–42. It has to earn its premium through stated construction facts, not an inflated list price. Coupons, which are excluded from the Typical Price recalculation, are the safer always-on discount tool. The collar's "available at a lower price from other sellers" notice also needs investigating, because a reseller or Amazon Resale offer winning the Featured Offer stops Leatherific's Sponsored Products ads and pulls down the plotted price ([Repricer.com](https://www.repricer.com/blog/amazon-buy-box-guide-2026/)).

## Traffic: ads build rank only when they convert

Amazon publishes no ranking formula. Its research and job postings describe learning-to-rank models that jointly optimise purchase, relevance and personalisation ([Amazon Science](https://amazon.science/publications/amazon-search-the-joy-of-ranking-products); [job posting](https://mygwork.com/jobs/amazon-applied-scientist-ii-search-ranking-6248980)). "A10" is vendor shorthand. The view that ads lift organic rank rests on uncontrolled case studies. Tinuiti reported one keyword moving from organic position #303 to #53 in 18 days ([Tinuiti](https://tinuiti.com/?p=6094)), and Perpetua reported +104% organic sales for one client ([Perpetua](https://perpetua.io/blog-organic-growth-sponsored-ads/)). The defensible reading is that ads help only insofar as they produce purchases on the target query. Claims that PPC sales count for less than organic sales, or that external traffic gets a special boost, are folklore.

For Leatherific, this argues for concentrating spend on the terms that already convert. Those are "spiked collar" (17% CVR), "spike collar for dogs" (10.8%), "spiked dog collar" (48 orders, 4.2%), "weight lifting belts" (10.5%), "leather lifting straps" (8.5%) and "weight lifting belt" (7.4%). The organic collar rank of about #48 for "spiked dog collar" shows the gap a converting exact-match push can close. "Leather tool belt" ($343 wasted) should become a negative. The leash's 61% ACoS mostly reflects a 1-image listing at 4.0 stars, so fix the page before adding spend. Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands prompts have been generally available in the US since 25 March 2026. They are auto-enrolled, billed per click, and can only be paused; the Prompts report lists the AI-generated prompt text with clicks, spend and sales ([Amazon Ads](https://advertising.amazon.com/resources/whats-new/unboxed-2025-sponsored-products-and-sponsored-brands-prompts)). Branded Conversations, which lets brands submit a conversation brief, launches in the US in January 2027 according to press ([eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/amazon-gives-brands-more-control-over-ai-shopping-conversations)). Leatherific should draft its differentiation brief now, because the same text strengthens its A+.

External traffic tagged through Amazon Attribution earns a Brand Referral Bonus that agencies put at about 10% of attributed sales, paid as a fee credit over a 14-day window. Amazon's official rate table was not read ([SellerMetrics](https://sellermetrics.app/amazon-brand-referral/)). Leatherific should treat it as a margin lever for email, Instagram and site traffic, not as a ranking trick.

Brand Analytics is the first-party measurement layer ([Adbrew](https://adbrew.io/blog/search-query-performance)). Search Query Performance covers the brand's top queries with impression, click, cart and purchase shares. Use it to find queries where Leatherific's purchase share beats its impression share, which signals relevance that is not yet visible. Search Catalog Performance flags ASINs with high impressions and low CTR, which points to the main image, price or rating rather than the text.

## AI discovery: Alexa for Shopping reads facts, and COSMO learns from purchases

On 13 May 2026, Amazon merged Rufus and Alexa+ into **Alexa for Shopping** for US customers on the app, the website and Echo Show. It brought search-bar questions, AI overviews in search and on product pages, side-by-side comparison, up to a year of price history, Scheduled Actions, Shop Direct/Buy for Me and custom shopping guides. Amazon says it draws on "deep product knowledge, in-depth information from across the web" plus each shopper's history and conversations ([About Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/retail/alexa-for-shopping-ai-assistant)). Amazon reported more than 350M users in the trailing 12 months on its Q2 2026 call ([Retail Dive](https://retaildive.com/news/amazon-alexa-for-shopping-agentic-ai-adoption/826806/)). At launch, Amazon named the original Rufus inputs as catalog data, reviews, community Q&A and web content ([Retail Insight Network](https://www.retail-insight-network.com/news/amazon-ai-shopping-assistant-rufus)). Amazon has published no weighting of individual fields. Agencies believe that A+, Brand Story and text inside images are read, and that the old Q&A list has been folded into the assistant; neither is confirmed ([AMALYTIX](https://www.amalytix.com/en/knowledge/ai/amazon-rufus-guide-2026/)). Canada has Rufus ([aboutamazon.ca](https://aboutamazon.ca/news/retail/amazon-announces-the-launch-of-rufus-a-new-generative-ai-powered-conversational-shopping-assistant-in-canada)), and no rename there is documented. No Australian launch was found.

COSMO is Amazon's large-scale commonsense knowledge graph. An LLM generates it from query-then-purchase and co-purchase pairs, and it links products to functions, audiences, events and locations through relations such as used_for_function, used_for_event and used_for_audience. Amazon reports up to a 60% macro-F1 gain in query relevance ([Amazon Science](https://www.amazon.science/blog/building-commonsense-knowledge-graphs-to-aid-product-recommendation); [paper](https://assets.amazon.science/19/5d/bde30d0d4019be6421e79e50cda9/cosmo-paper.pdf)). Amazon has never stated in writing that COSMO powers Rufus or Alexa; that link is agency inference ([ZonGuru](https://www.zonguru.com/blog/how-amazon-rufus-recommends-products)).

The practical consequence for Leatherific is to state, in plain text, the function, audience, occasion and pairing for every product. Examples: "for squats and deadlifts", "for strong pullers at curbs", "nightstand catchall", "housewarming gift", "pairs with matching leash". This gives retrieval and the LLM explicit hooks. Purchases on those intent queries then reinforce the graph. Every claim that appears only in an infographic should also appear in text. Contradictions such as brass versus alloy versus glued are the worst case for an assistant asked "are the spikes real brass?" or "will the spikes fall off?"; AU reviews already flag spike durability (8 mentions) and CA durability runs 8 of 31 reviews negative.

## Category notes: leather law and sport rules restrict the claims

**All leather goods.** The FTC Leather Guides (16 CFR Part 24) prohibit misrepresenting the composition of a product or any part of it. They require disclosure of hidden backings and of embossing that imitates another leather, and they restrict "waterproof"-type terms ([16 CFR 24](https://chanrobles.com/usa/uslaws/cfr/title16/16-1.0.1.2.14.php)). An unqualified "Made in USA" claim needs all or virtually all US content. The FTC warned Amazon about seller origin claims in 2025 ([FTC letter](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/amazon-musa-letter.pdf)). Amazon's environmental-claims policy requires FTC Green Guides compliance ([Seller Central G201893650](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G201893650)), so the collar's "eco-friendly" should go. Words such as "non-toxic", "safe" and "antibacterial" can trip Amazon's automated pesticide classification, according to sellers and lawyers ([Amazon Sellers Lawyer](https://amazonsellerslawyer.com/blog/epa-regulations-how-amazon-sellers-avoid-pesticide-violations/)). Another brand's name, such as "Apple" on the mouse pad, is allowed only in the "compatible with" format and only when true ([Seller Forums](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/e4c041c3fa577b6a3f5ebd9352c8487c)). For a mouse pad it adds nothing, so remove it.

**Dog collars and leashes.** Sizing best practice is neck circumference measured with the dog standing, plus the two-finger rule ([AKC](https://shop.akc.org/pages/the-ultimate-dog-apparel-measuring-guide)). Agencies suggest an extra 3–4 cm allowance for thick leather ([Rogue Royalty](https://rogueroyalty.com.au/blogs/news/how-to-measure-dog-for-collar-australia)). Studded-collar complaints cluster at rivet failure ([Rogue Royalty](https://rogueroyalty.com.au/blogs/news/studded-leather-dog-collars-australia)), which matches the A+ statement that the spikes are glued. Leatherific should state the true fixing method, show the inside of the collar, and add responsible-use language ("remove when crated"). Avoid "chew-proof", "escape-proof" and "100% safe". Competing braided double-handle leashes sell at $16–35 with titles of the form material, length × width, "Traffic Handle", dog size ([Amazon SERP](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=braided+leather+dog+leash+double+handle)). At $38.99, the Leatherific leash needs its width, thickness and hardware stated.

**Lifting belts and straps.** Competitor leather belts sell at $27–56 with titles built from brand, material, "4 Inch", thickness, buckle type, lifts and "Men Women". Nylon Element 26 competes on the same query ([Amazon SERP](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=leather+weight+lifting+belt)). Federation limits are reported as 10 cm wide and 13 mm thick, with approval granted per listed model ([PowerliftingTechnique](https://powerliftingtechnique.com/ipf-usapl-approved-equipment/)). 4 in is 10.16 cm, slightly over a 10 cm cap. Unless Leatherific appears on the IPF, USAPL, USPA and IPL lists, the "meets IPF/USAPL/USPA/IPL standards" claim should go, replaced with exact dimensions and "check your federation's rules". "Top-rated" and "Trusted worldwide" are promotional claims and should go too. Belt reviews across brands complain about stiffness and hole spacing ([GarageGymReviews](https://garagegymreviews.com/gymreapers-lever-belt-review)), so a break-in bullet and a waist-based chart pre-empt the obvious returns. Leather straps are stiffer and more durable than cotton or nylon ([Tuff Wraps](https://www.tuffwraps.com/blogs/news/leather-vs-nylon-vs-cotton-lifting-straps)).

**Mouse pads, coasters and valet trays.** Edge curl is mainly a faux-leather complaint, and leather scratches but develops a patina ([Parkablogs](https://www.parkablogs.com/index.php/content/review-galen-leather-mouse-pad)). The mouse pad's base must be named accurately, because a hidden backing is an FTC disclosure item. Naked full-grain coasters absorb condensation and darken, while sealed leather pools water ([Buffalo Billfold](https://buffalobillfoldcompany.com/do-leather-coasters-work/)). State which applies and never say "waterproof". Valet trays are sold as men's gifts at $10–27, mostly in PU ([Walmart](https://www.walmart.com/ip/1120653931)). Leatherific's real-leather claim, measurable walls and gift use cases are the differentiators, and "Dice" and "Bathroom" tray types dilute them.

## Marketplace notes: US, Canada and Australia differ

**United States.** All of the 2026 changes above apply: the 75-character title and Item Highlights, Alexa for Shopping, Sponsored Prompts, List Price and Typical Price rules, and 365-day price history. This is where to test.

**Canada.** No Amazon.ca rule requiring French listing text was found. Quebec's Bill 96 and the federal labelling law expose packaging, inserts and care cards more than listings ([CFIB](https://www.cfib-fcei.ca/en/site/qc-law-14-bill-96); [Weglot](https://www.weglot.com/blog/bill-96-explained)). Leatherific should print EN/FR care cards and check the auto-translated French pages, which sellers report as unreliable ([Seller Forums CA](https://sellercentral.amazon.ca/seller-forums/discussions/t/bbf5bb7e-26b6-4099-93b1-1b384b61300a)). Build International Listings syncs offers and prices by exchange rate and re-syncs when the US price moves more than 1% ([ChannelX](https://channelx.world/2021/02/amazon-build-international-listings-pricing-rules-now-in-automate-pricing/)). That means a US deal price can carry into CA unless a rule blocks it. CA prices currently sit at about 2.0–2.15 times US (mouse pad CA$31.95 vs $15.99; coasters CA$39.85 vs $19.99; leash CA$83.83 vs $38.99), well above any exchange-rate conversion. Review them against CA competitors. A+ and Brand Story are available on .ca under Brand Registry ([sell.amazon.ca](https://sell.amazon.ca/tools/a-content)). Count bytes, not characters, for French backend terms. The shopping assistant is still branded Rufus. CA 2026 deal deadlines were not found; check Seller Central CA today.

**Australia.** One global Brand Registry account covers AU. Amazon accepts a pending or registered mark from any office in a country with an Amazon store, so a USPTO mark suffices, though Amazon recommends filing locally too ([sell.amazon.com.au](https://sell.amazon.com.au/brand-registry)). Some sellers report rejections while an IP Australia application was pending, so enrol on the US mark. Enrolling unlocks A+, Stores, Sponsored Brands, Brand Analytics, Vine and the New Seller Incentive: a 5% referral credit on the first A$1.5M of branded sales plus a A$75 ads credit. Prices must be shown GST-inclusive ([Maropost](https://galaxy.maropost.com/s/article/amazon-australia-and-gst-goods-and-services-tax)). No Rufus or Alexa launch in AU was found, so classic search hygiene and stock depth matter most there.

## Alexa for Shopping monthly test protocol

No API or official report covers organic assistant visibility ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/rufus-shows-5-products-not-50-what-brands-must-know-about-amazons-ai-filter/)). Testing therefore means a fixed manual panel, run on the same days each month. Responses are personalised and non-deterministic, so read the results as trends, never as single observations. The protocol below is synthesised from agency methods ([AMALYTIX](https://www.amalytix.com/en/knowledge/ai/amazon-rufus-guide-2026/)) and adapted to Leatherific.

| Step | What to do | Example for Leatherific |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Fixed panel | 20–30 prompts per family, split across informational, comparative, transactional, use-case or audience, and own-ASIN prompts | "best spiked collar for a pit bull", "leather vs nylon lifting belt", "leather valet tray under $30", "gift for a lifter who has everything", "are Leatherific collar spikes real brass" |
| 2. Two contexts | Run each prompt signed in and in a clean account, on the app and on desktop (US). Run CA as Rufus. | Same prompts every month |
| 3. Log | Whether Leatherific is named, its position among about 5 named products, which claims are quoted, and any factual errors | One spreadsheet row per prompt per month |
| 4. Own-ASIN audit | Ask the assistant on each product page: "what is it made of", "who is it for", "downsides", "how does it fit" | Every wrong or vague answer becomes a content fix in attributes, bullets or A+ |
| 5. Category criteria | Capture the AI overview text at the top of search for the head terms and check the listing states a value for every criterion it names | "spiked dog collar", "weight lifting belt", "leather mouse pad" |
| 6. Prompts report | Download the Sponsored Prompts report; pause off-target or claim-risky prompts; note the questions Amazon generates | Shows what Amazon's AI thinks each listing is selling |
| 7. One change at a time | Change one variable, then re-run the panel 2–4 weeks later | For example, attributes only on the belt in January |
| 8. Gates first | Do not invest in AI copy for any ASIN below about 4.0 stars or out of stock until that is fixed | Mouse pad and coasters wait for Vine reviews |

## Open questions for the seller

Most of the copy above depends on these answers. Each one also affects an attribute and the A+.

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| What are the spikes made of on each colour (solid brass, plated alloy, other), and are they riveted, screw-back or glued? | Brass, alloy and glued currently contradict each other. It drives the returns badge risk, durability reviews and FTC accuracy. |
| What is the leather grade and thickness for the collar, leash, belt and straps? Full-grain, top-grain or genuine (split)? | The titles, bullets and the 16 CFR 24 description all depend on it, as does the thickness attribute conflict. |
| Is the belt 8 mm or 10 mm, exactly 4 in or 10 cm, and what buckle type and hole spacing does it have? Is the brand on any federation's approved list? | The title figure, the federation claim and the size chart all depend on it. |
| How wide are the wrist straps, what loop type do they have, how long are they, and are they sold as a pair? | The strap title and bullets have placeholders for these. |
| What is the mouse pad's base (leather, suede or felt), and is it 8 x 10 or 10 x 8.2 in? | FTC backing disclosure and the size conflict. |
| Is the coaster leather sealed or naked, what is the holder made of, and what does "holders without coasters" mean? | Condensation claims and the misleading bullet. |
| Are the offers fulfilled by FBA in each marketplace? | Vine requires FBA, and Prime status affects Buy Box and assistant visibility. |
| Who is the cheaper seller on the US collar, and is it a used or Resale offer? | Buy Box loss stops ads and lowers the price history. |
| What is the country of origin, and is "handmade" accurate? | Consistency across the attribute, copy and packaging. |
| Does the USPTO trademark cover the Leatherific word mark, so AU can be enrolled? | Unlocks A+, Brand Story and Vine in AU. |

## Conclusion

The research changes what "optimisation" means for a small leather brand in late 2026. Keyword packing has lost most of its room: the title now holds 75 characters, the bullets lost their emojis, A+ text is probably not indexed, and alt text is being automated. What Amazon's systems now reward is a listing whose facts are complete, consistent across every field and marketplace, and true to the physical product, backed by a rating above about 4.0 and steady stock. Leatherific's main weakness is not missing keywords but contradictions that confuse shoppers, the assistant and the returns metrics alike. The same edit (one true spike-metal answer, one correct size story, one product per variation family) fixes compliance, returns and AI visibility at once.

The sequencing follows from the gates. Fix the facts and titles before the freeze. Spend November protecting stock, Buy Box and ad efficiency rather than editing. Use January's quiet period for Vine on the five under-30-review parents, MYE on the collar and belt, and the first monthly Alexa panel. Branded Conversations arrives in January, so the differentiation story Leatherific writes for A+ now will also serve as its brief for the next ad format.
