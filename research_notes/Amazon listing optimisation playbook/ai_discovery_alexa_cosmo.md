# Amazon AI Discovery Layer: Alexa for Shopping (ex-Rufus), COSMO and AI ad formats (as of 17 Oct 2026)

Source labels used below: **[AMAZON]** = Amazon's own statement (aboutamazon.com, amazon.science, advertising.amazon.com, earnings calls). **[PRESS]** = trade or financial press reporting. **[AGENCY/VENDOR]** = agency or tool-vendor observation or opinion (treat as directional, not confirmed).

## 1. What Amazon officially announced: Alexa for Shopping (13 May 2026) and later features; marketplace differences

### Takeaway
On 13 May 2026 Amazon merged Rufus with Alexa+ into "Alexa for Shopping" for **U.S.** customers on the app, the website and Echo Show. The launch included search-bar Q&A, AI overviews in search and on product pages, side-by-side comparison, up to 12 months of price history, Scheduled Actions, Shop Direct/Buy for Me, Custom shopping guides and visual search. Outside the U.S., Amazon has published nothing about a rename. Canada had Rufus, and I found no evidence of an Australian launch.

It would "roll out to all U.S. customers over the coming week". It is free when signed in, with no Prime or Echo needed. — [aboutamazon.com, Meet Alexa for Shopping](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/retail/alexa-for-shopping-ai-assistant)
- [AMAZON] Data inputs as Amazon describes them: it "combines deep product knowledge, in-depth information from across the web, and powerful shopping capabilities with your personal preferences, shopping history, and conversations from across both Amazon.com and Alexa". ### Inferences
- In the U.S., all customer-facing AI surfaces should be treated as one system ("Alexa for Shopping"). In Canada, sellers should expect the "Rufus" label and the older feature set until Amazon.ca announces otherwise.
- Shop Direct and Buy for Me let the assistant send shoppers to other stores. A brand's own website can therefore be surfaced inside Amazon's assistant, which gives the D2C site's product data a reason to be clean as well.
- A visible 12-month price history makes inflated "list price" anchors and pre-sale price hikes easy for shoppers to spot. Pricing should stay consistent.

### Gaps
- I found no Amazon statement confirming whether Canada (or the UK and other markets) moved to the "Alexa for Shopping" name. AMALYTIX says other locales "may still show Rufus".
- I found no source confirming any Rufus or Alexa for Shopping availability on amazon.com.au. Searches returned only the 2024 U.S. launch coverage.
- I did not find an Amazon page with a precise launch date for the 365-day view. Amazon's 13 May post already says "up to a full year", while EcomCrew dates it to 30 Jun.

## 2. What data sources it uses (titles, bullets, attributes, A+, Brand Story, image text, alt text, reviews, Q&A, price, availability)

### Takeaway
Amazon has confirmed these inputs: catalog and product knowledge, customer reviews, community Q&A, information from across the web, price history, and the shopper's own history, preferences and conversations. Amazon has not documented how much each listing field counts. Agencies consistently say all PDP fields, A+ and Brand Story, and text inside images are read. They also say the old public Q&A list has been folded into the assistant, but that is supported only by forum anecdotes.

### Cited Findings
- [AMAZON] At the 2024 Rufus launch, Amazon said it draws on "Amazon's catalog data, customer reviews, and Q&As as well as information from across the web". — [Retail Insight Network](https://www.retail-insight-network.com/news/amazon-ai-shopping-assistant-rufus); [Maginative](https://www.maginative.com/article/amazon-launches-rufus-your-ai-shopping-assistant/)
- [AMAZON] In 2026 Amazon describes the inputs as "deep product knowledge, in-depth information from across the web … personal preferences, shopping history, and conversations". — [aboutamazon.com](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/retail/alexa-for-shopping-ai-assistant)
- [PRESS, citing Amazon Science/AWS ML blog] Rufus was trained on the full Amazon catalog, customer reviews, community Q&A and curated public web content. It is a RAG system: a query-planner model classifies intent, then retrieval pulls from the catalog, reviews, Q&A and internal Stores APIs. It runs on Trainium/Inferentia, with more than 80,000 chips allocated for Prime Day. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/rufus-shows-5-products-not-50-what-brands-must-know-about-amazons-ai-filter/)
- [AMAZON, Seller Forum moderator] When a seller asked about the missing Q&A, the moderator pointed to the Rufus help page, which says Rufus draws on community Q&As, reviews and product pages. In the same thread, the seller reported that the old per-ASIN unanswered-questions link no longer works. — [Seller Central EU forum](https://sellercentral-europe.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/abf1ebc7-aa70-41bb-b6bd-aea8206ffdf2)
- [ANECDOTAL] Shoppers report that the static Q&A list is gone from PDPs on desktop and app. Human Q&A is reportedly reached through the "Looking for specific info?" box and a "Show related customer Q&As and reviews" link under the AI answer. — [Motley Fool discussion board](https://discussion.fool.com/t/amazon-continues-to-frustrate-consumers-looking-for-product-info/124607) (unverified forum posts)
- [AGENCY] AMALYTIX (May 2026) lists these data sources:
  - titles, bullets, descriptions, technical specs and variants;
  - price and shipping, including price history;
  - reviews, Q&A and rating context;
  - A+ (including Premium A+ and Brand Story);
  - images ("processing text overlays and infographics");
  - user signals (purchases, views, wishlist, account, chat history);
  - external web sources, mainly for high-consideration, technical, safety-critical, niche or time-sensitive queries.

  — [AMALYTIX](https://www.amalytix.com/en/knowledge/ai/amazon-rufus-guide-2026/)
- [AGENCY] Workflow Labs (via PPC Land) argues that COSMO reads structured backend attributes such as `target_gender`, `age_range_description`, `material_type`, `intended_use`, `special_feature` and `fabric_type` "more heavily than the front-end copy". It says "most brands leave roughly half of them empty". This is opinion built on secondary research, and Amazon has not confirmed it. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/rufus-shows-5-products-not-50-what-brands-must-know-about-amazons-ai-filter/)
- [AGENCY] Agencies say image text is read by OCR or vision-language models: infographics with readable text feed the assistant, while lifestyle photos alone do not. One vendor admits image parsing "isn't perfect". A "hidden off-colour keywords in main image" hack has been floated but is untested. — [imagine.io](https://resources.imagine.io/blog/how-amazon-rufus-uses-image-recognition-to-enhance-product-discovery); [findskill.ai](https://findskill.ai/learn/amazon-rufus/); [Norfolk Daily News/BDSN](https://norfolkdailynews.com/online_features/press_releases/bdsn-does-this-amazon-rufus-image-hack-work/article_992e7e34-3c34-5e38-aeea-187ec450a772.html)
- [AMAZON, 2022, may be legacy] "Customers ask Alexa" was an invite-only Seller Central program in which brands answered customer questions. The answers were attributed to the brand and were not sponsored. — [aboutamazon.com](https://aboutamazon.com/news/small-business/alexa-can-now-help-brands-answer-customer-questions)
- [PRESS] In April 2025 Amazon announced two-part product titles that separate core identification from marketing highlights. PPC Land frames this as helping AI systems parse structured data. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/rufus-shows-5-products-not-50-what-brands-must-know-about-amazons-ai-filter/)

### Inferences
- The inputs that are confirmed and that the seller controls are catalog content (all PDP text plus attributes), reviews and Q&A (indirectly), price, and, through Shop Direct and the web, the brand's off-Amazon content.
- Shoppers can no longer browse an open Q&A list to see questions. Sellers should therefore answer the obvious questions in bullets, A+ and the attribute fields themselves, rather than rely on community Q&A.
- Because image text may or may not be parsed reliably, every key claim shown in an infographic should also appear in text fields.

### Gaps
- I found no Amazon statement on whether image alt text in A+ is read, or whether alt text is now AI-generated.
- I found no Amazon confirmation that the community Q&A section was removed from PDPs, and no removal date.
- No source quantifies how much each field (attributes vs bullets vs A+) contributes. The claim that backend attributes dominate is agency opinion.
- I found no Amazon statement on whether the returns badge or "frequently returned" flags are visible to or used by the AI.

## 3. Observed filters and hygiene: under-4.0-star exclusion, out-of-stock, other floors. How reliable?

### Takeaway
Three independent observational studies (AMALYTIX Nov 2024, Mars United/Profitero 2026, and AMALYTIX's 2026 guide) consistently find that products below about 4.0 stars are almost never recommended and that recommended items are about 98% in stock. These are correlations from samples, not Amazon policy. The returns-badge claims have no credible support.

### Cited Findings
- [AGENCY] AMALYTIX, Nov 2024: more than 1,300 Rufus-recommended products across 500 top U.S. search terms. Profile of the recommended set:
  - essentially none below 4.0 stars; median rating 4.5; median review count 2,991;
  - titles average 166 characters, many at the 199 maximum;
  - median 7 images, median 3 videos, though 34% had no video at all; median 5 bullets;
  - A+ type: 55.1% standard, 32.1% premium, 12.8% basic;
  - 94.2% FBA, 92.1% Prime-eligible, 98.4% in stock;
  - Amazon Basics only 0.6%, and Amazon's Choice badge on 41.8%.

  — [PPC Land summary](https://ppc.land/rufus-shows-5-products-not-50-what-brands-must-know-about-amazons-ai-filter/); [AMALYTIX pattern analysis](https://www.amalytix.com/en/knowledge/ai/amazon-rufus-pattern-analysis/)
- [AGENCY] Mars United Commerce with Profitero ("Decoding Rufus", Mar 2026): more than 1,000 recommended products from about 300 prompts. Rufus only recommended products rated 4 stars or higher, and products with low review counts were rarely recommended. — [Mars United](https://www.marsunited.com/what-makes-rufus-tick-insights-into-amazons-agentic-search-results/)
- [AGENCY] AMALYTIX 2026 guide: "Products below 4.0 stars are typically not recommended, and availability acts as a hard filter." — [AMALYTIX](https://www.amalytix.com/en/knowledge/ai/amazon-rufus-guide-2026/)
- [AGENCY] Workflow Labs (Apr 2026) says AI answers name about 5 products, compared with roughly 50 on a results page. It quotes Profitero audits finding that more than 50% of brands' live content was wrong. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/rufus-shows-5-products-not-50-what-brands-must-know-about-amazons-ai-filter/)
- [AGENCY, flagged as unverified] Workflow Labs could not trace the widely repeated claim that listings with 15+ Q&As appear 3.2x more often to any primary study. The "20–35% conversion lift" and "3–5x CTR" claims also have no traceable source. A knife-listing case study (+41% sessions) is a single seller with no control group. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/rufus-shows-5-products-not-50-what-brands-must-know-about-amazons-ai-filter/)

### Inferences
- In practice, about 4.0 stars works as a gate. A listing at 3.8–3.9 should fix product issues and review velocity (for example via Vine) before investing in AI-focused copy.
- Stock-outs probably remove an ASIN from AI answers. The 98.4% in-stock rate among recommended items fits a filter, but it is also what any popular product set would show.
- Under ±0.1 stars of measurement noise, the line between 4.0 and 3.9 is fuzzy. Treat the threshold as a cut-off of "approximately 4.0".

### Gaps
- Amazon has not stated any star or review threshold.
- I found no evidence that the returns badge ("frequently returned item") affects AI recommendations, so that claim is unsupported.
- No study isolates out-of-stock exclusion with a before/after design.

## 4. COSMO: what the SIGMOD 2024 paper and Amazon Science blog say, and the implications for copy

### Takeaway
COSMO is Amazon's LLM-built commonsense knowledge graph. It is mined from query→purchase and co-purchase behaviour and links products to human contexts: functions, audiences, events, locations. Amazon says it improves query-product relevance by up to 60% (macro F1, frozen encoders). Amazon has never said in print that "COSMO powers Rufus/Alexa". That link is agency inference.

### Cited Findings
- [AMAZON] Purpose: deduce commonsense needs, for example that "shoes for pregnant women" implies slip-resistant shoes. The graph encodes "relationships between products … and the human contexts in which they play a role — their functions, their audiences, the locations in which they're used". Example relation: `used_for_audience` linking slip-resistant shoes and pregnant women. — [Amazon Science blog, 10 May 2024](https://www.amazon.science/blog/building-commonsense-knowledge-graphs-to-aid-product-recommendation)
- [AMAZON] How it is built:
  - Inputs are **query-purchase pairs** (a query plus a purchase within a fixed time or click window) and **co-purchase pairs** (purchases in the same session). Pairs whose categories are too far apart are pruned.
  - An LLM labels each pair with one of four seed relations: **usedFor, capableOf, isA, cause**.
  - Amazon then culls finer-grained recurring relations into canonical forms, for example **used_for_function, used_for_event, used_for_audience**, and re-prompts the LLM with the larger set.
  - Heuristics filter out empty rationales and paraphrases. Human annotators score plausibility and typicality, and a classifier trained on those scores thresholds the remaining candidates.
  - Instructions built from the validated principles are used to re-prompt the LLM.
  - Output is triples such as <co-purchase of camera case and screen protector, capableOf, protecting camera>, or "winter coat" → long-sleeve puffer → capableOf "provide high-level warmth".

  — [Amazon Science blog](https://www.amazon.science/blog/building-commonsense-knowledge-graphs-to-aid-product-recommendation)
- [AMAZON] Evaluation on the KDD Cup 2022 Shopping Queries Data Set:
  - With frozen encoders, a COSMO-augmented cross-encoder beat the best baseline by 60% on macro F1.
  - After fine-tuning, the COSMO-augmented model kept a 28% edge on macro F1 and 22% on micro F1.

  — [Amazon Science blog](https://www.amazon.science/blog/building-commonsense-knowledge-graphs-to-aid-product-recommendation)
- [AMAZON] The paper is "COSMO: A Large-Scale E-commerce Common Sense Knowledge Generation and Serving System at Amazon", SIGMOD-Companion '24, Santiago. It motivates the work with intentions missing from attribute graphs (for example, buying clothes "to attend a wedding"). Its method is LLM seed assertions, then critic classifiers trained on human annotations, then an instruction-tuned COSMO-LM for scale. — [Amazon paper PDF](https://assets.amazon.science/19/5d/bde30d0d4019be6421e79e50cda9/cosmo-paper.pdf)
- [SECONDARY, unverified against paper] Third-party figures, which I could not check against the paper:
  - 18 major categories and about 30k annotated instructions; deployed in search relevance, session-based recommendation and search navigation ([VoltageDM](https://voltagedm.substack.com/p/cosmo-amazon-fba-search-engine-optimization));
  - 6.3M nodes and 29M edges, about 0.7% sales uplift in A/B tests, and "15 relation types" ([SmartScout](https://www.smartscout.com/blog/amazon-cosmo-explained); [ZonGuru](https://www.zonguru.com/blog/cosmo-semantic-relationship-types)).
- [AGENCY] ZonGuru notes that Amazon "has not published a single document explicitly stating 'COSMO powers Rufus'". — [ZonGuru](https://www.zonguru.com/blog/how-amazon-rufus-recommends-products)
- [AGENCY] Workflow Labs' model is three layers: A10 (lexical/behavioural ranking), then COSMO (intent/semantic, reading structured attributes), then Rufus/Alexa (conversational articulation using reviews, Q&A and coherence). This is a framework, not an Amazon disclosure. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/rufus-shows-5-products-not-50-what-brands-must-know-about-amazons-ai-filter/)

### Inferences
- COSMO learns relations from **what shoppers searched and then bought**. Copy cannot directly "insert" a product into the graph. It can, however, make the product's function, audience, event or occasion, and location or conditions explicit, so that retrieval and LLM layers match the product to those contexts. Examples: "for long shifts on hard floors", "gift for new dads", "for wet trailheads".
- Write use cases in the relation vocabulary Amazon uses: used for [function], used for [event], used for [audience], capable of [outcome], is a [type]. Make complementary items explicit, since co-purchase relations exist (for example "pairs with X", "fits Y").
- Because the graph is built from conversions, sales velocity on intent-specific queries likely reinforces relevance. Ads that generate purchases for a use-case query may indirectly strengthen that association. This is speculative.

### Gaps
- I could not read the full PDF: Firecrawl's antibot blocked the PDF fetch, and curl was blocked by the proxy. The exact full relation list, the graph scale and the online A/B results in the paper remain unverified here.
- Amazon has published nothing documenting COSMO's role in Rufus or Alexa for Shopping specifically.

## 5. Sponsored Prompts (GA 25 Mar 2026), Branded Conversations (unBoxed, Sep 2026) and the Prompts report

### Takeaway
SP and SB prompts are AI-generated, auto-enrolled ad extensions billed per click. The only controls are pausing individual prompts and reading a prompt-level report. Advertisers can't write or bid on prompts themselves. Branded Conversations, launching in the U.S. in January 2027, adds a brand-written "conversation brief" that steers sponsored Alexa conversations. It is labelled sponsored and reported at prompt level.

### Cited Findings
- [AMAZON] Amazon Ads launch page (10 Mar 2026):
  - SP and SB prompts moved "from open beta to general availability in the U.S. on March 25, 2026".
  - Prompts "can appear in shopping results and product detail pages", and when clicked "may open a dialog in Rufus or respond to the customer directly on the page".
  - They "leverage Amazon's first-party signals from your detail pages, Brand Store, campaign data, and more".
  - Campaigns are "automatically enrolled … leverage existing campaign parameters (e.g., campaign targeting) without any additional setup required".
  - Charged "as part of your CPC bidding and billing parameters".
  - "If you'd like to pause a prompt, you can do so at any time".
  - U.S. only, and excludes authors and publishers.

  — [Amazon Ads](https://advertising.amazon.com/resources/whats-new/unboxed-2025-sponsored-products-and-sponsored-brands-prompts)
- [AMAZON] Where to manage them:
  - Campaign → Ad Group → Ads → **Prompts tab**, which lists prompts "if they have received a click".
  - **Prompts report**: Reports → Create report → category Sponsored Products → type Prompts. Fields are prompt text, associated ad, impressions, clicks, CTR, CPC, spend, sales, ACOS, ROAS, and 7-day orders and units.
  - Also available via API.

  — [Amazon Ads](https://advertising.amazon.com/resources/whats-new/unboxed-2025-sponsored-products-and-sponsored-brands-prompts)
- [PRESS] Prompts were introduced as a free beta at unBoxed on 11 Nov 2025. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/amazons-ai-shopping-prompts-are-now-billable-what-changed-on-march-25/)
- [PRESS/AGENCY] Amazon pre-determines eligible prompts from PDP content. Brands can opt out of specific prompts but cannot create custom prompts or allocate a dedicated budget. — [Intentwise](https://www.intentwise.com/blog/explained-sponsored-prompts-ads-on-amazon); [Acadia](https://acadia.io/advertising-in-the-age-of-rufus-breaking-down-sponsored-product-prompts)
- [PRESS] Performance data is thin. The Information, via eMarketer, reported one midsized seller with 88 prompt clicks against 500,000 clicks across all its Amazon ads, at about half the CPC. A seller-focused source claims early prompt CPCs run 15–30% above standard SP. These conflict, and neither is a benchmark. — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/rufus-ads-open-window-amazon-s-ai-while-missing-some-shoppers); [Novadata](https://novadata.io/resources/news/amazon-rufus-sponsored-prompts-cpc-billing-march-2026)
- [PRESS] Branded Conversations (eMarketer, 29 Sep 2026):
  - Unveiled at unBoxed 2026 (San Francisco, 28–30 Sep) and launches in the U.S. in January 2027.
  - It extends SB prompts.
  - Advertisers submit a "conversation brief" covering differentiation versus competitors, how products compare, which customer needs they address, and bundles or routines. Amazon layers this onto PDP, Brand Store and other data.
  - Responses are labelled sponsored, with a disclosure that brand-provided information shaped them.
  - Briefs can be edited mid-campaign.
  - Reporting shows **sample Alexa conversations about the brand**, plus prompt-level clicks, conversions and ROAS.

  — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/amazon-gives-brands-more-control-over-ai-shopping-conversations); [eMarketer unBoxed](https://www.emarketer.com/content/amazon-unboxed-puts-brand-authority-center-of-ai-powered-shopping)
- [AMAZON via PRESS] eMarketer reports Jassy as saying on the Q2 2026 call that shoppers who click a sponsored prompt "convert 48% more often and spend 21% more on average". I could not find this in other Q2 coverage, so verify it against the transcript. — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/amazon-gives-brands-more-control-over-ai-shopping-conversations)
- [AGENCY] One analysis says pricing, whether competitor products can appear in Branded Conversations, and Japan plans were not disclosed. — [Stellagent](https://stellagent.ai/insights/amazon-branded-conversations-alexa-shopping-ads)
- [PRESS] unBoxed 2026 also launched a "Review Request" beta. — [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/opinion/amazon-just-unveiled-an-ai-arsenal-brands-were-more-excited-by-new-reviews)
- [AGENCY] AMALYTIX recommends downloading the Prompts report, reviewing the generated questions, disabling mismatched prompts, and reinforcing useful ones with consistent PDP messaging. — [AMALYTIX](https://www.amalytix.com/en/knowledge/ai/amazon-rufus-guide-2026/)

### Inferences
- Prompts are generated from PDP and Brand Store content. Reading the prompt text in the report is a free view of what Amazon's AI thinks a product's selling points and likely questions are. It doubles as an audit of the listing.
- Any SP or SB spend now includes prompt placements by default. Pausing irrelevant or claim-risky prompts is a routine hygiene step.
- Branded Conversations will reward brands with a crisp, defensible differentiation story. Write it now, because the same text also strengthens organic PDP and A+ content.

### Gaps
- Amazon's own page still says "Rufus". Whether prompts now open Alexa for Shopping in every case is unconfirmed by Amazon.
- There is no official Amazon Ads page for Branded Conversations in my results, only press coverage. Pricing is undisclosed.
- I found no campaign-level opt-out for prompts.

## 6. How to test listings in Alexa for Shopping, and evidence on what content gets cited

### Takeaway
There is no API or official visibility report for organic AI answers. Testing means structured manual prompt panels, plus third-party trackers that work by scraping or simulation, and reading the Sponsored Prompts report and the SERP AI modules. Evidence on which content gets cited is observational. It shows strong ratings and review counts, complete A+, many images, FBA/Prime, in-stock status and explicit use cases.

### Cited Findings
- [PRESS/AGENCY] "There is no Rufus API". Amazon has blocked 47 AI bots, and every visibility tool relies on workarounds:
  - Stackline AI Visibility (Jan 2026): platform data plus query observation;
  - Azoma: simulated app or web queries;
  - Profitero/Mars United: a prompt-panel method;
  - Tinuiti/Profound: cross-platform citation tracking.

  — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/rufus-shows-5-products-not-50-what-brands-must-know-about-amazons-ai-filter/)
- [AGENCY] AMALYTIX lists the on-site surfaces to monitor:
  - the desktop search bar, where Alexa prompts appear before the results page;
  - **"Researched by AI"**, a SERP module;
  - **"Customers Ask"**, a SERP module with a generated short summary and a product carousel;
  - **"Ask Alexa"**, a pop-up on PDPs when text is highlighted;
  - **"Why you might like this"**, a personalised PDP summary;
  - **"Compare with similar items"**, an in-cart module;
  - **Custom Guide**, Shop direct, price alerts and visual search;
  - AI-generated Sponsored Prompts.

  It recommends using Researched by AI and Customers Ask as research inputs for question and comparison criteria. — [AMALYTIX](https://www.amalytix.com/en/knowledge/ai/amazon-rufus-guide-2026/)
- [AMAZON] The official equivalents are AI overviews at the top of search results and on PDPs, comparison of products selected from search, and custom shopping guides. — [aboutamazon.com](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/retail/alexa-for-shopping-ai-assistant)
- [AGENCY] An intent framework from Atomic AMZ and Podean, reported but not confirmed:
  - informational queries weight review text and Q&A;
  - comparative ("X vs Y") queries weight structured specs;
  - transactional ("best X under $50") queries weight price, Prime, the ratings floor and Buy Box.

  — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/rufus-shows-5-products-not-50-what-brands-must-know-about-amazons-ai-filter/)
- [AGENCY] Tinuiti: 10-word conversational queries trigger AI responses about 68% of the time, compared with 15% for single-word queries. Intentwise: Rufus was 13.7% of searches in October 2024. Workflow Labs: 38% of Black Friday 2025 sessions involved Rufus. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/rufus-shows-5-products-not-50-what-brands-must-know-about-amazons-ai-filter/)
- [AGENCY] AMALYTIX recommends:
  - make use cases, target users and problems explicit;
  - treat title, bullets, description and A+ as one semantic system;
  - put more information into A+ and images;
  - manage rating hygiene (for example with Vine) to stay above 4.0;
  - build category relevance through external mentions.

  — [AMALYTIX](https://www.amalytix.com/en/knowledge/ai/amazon-rufus-guide-2026/)
- [AMAZON] Seller-side recourse: when a forum seller raised incorrect answers, the moderator pointed to the Rufus help page and the feedback path for reporting them. — [Seller Central EU forum](https://sellercentral-europe.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/abf1ebc7-aa70-41bb-b6bd-aea8206ffdf2)

### Inferences
A practical test protocol, synthesised from the above:
1. Build a fixed panel of 20–40 prompts per ASIN, split into informational, comparative, transactional, use-case/audience ("for X person/occasion"), and "vs competitor" prompts.
2. Run the panel signed in and in a clean account, on the app and on desktop, monthly. Log whether the ASIN is named, its position, which claims are quoted, and any factual errors.
3. Capture the Researched by AI / Customers Ask module text for head terms. These modules show the comparison criteria the AI uses, so check that the listing states a value for each criterion.
4. Ask the assistant about your own ASIN, for example "what is this made of / is it good for X / downsides". Every wrong or vague answer marks a content gap to fix in attributes, bullets or A+.
5. Mine the Sponsored Prompts report text monthly.
6. Change one variable at a time (for example, fill backend attributes only) and re-run the panel after two to four weeks. Results will be noisy because responses are personalised and non-deterministic.

### Gaps
- No controlled, published study isolates the causal effect of any single content change on AI citation.
- I found no Amazon documentation of the "Researched by AI", "Customers Ask" or "Why you might like this" labels. Those names come from AMALYTIX observation.

## 7. Off-Amazon AI (ChatGPT shopping, Google AI Overviews/AI Mode, Perplexity) citing Amazon listings or brand sites

### Takeaway
The evidence conflicts and comes from vendors. The newest large study (LLM Pulse, Apr–Jul 2026) finds ChatGPT almost never cites amazon.com, and that Amazon's AI citations come mostly from Google surfaces. BrightEdge's 2025 holiday data found the opposite for ChatGPT. Amazon blocks many AI crawlers, so brand sites and third-party reviews probably matter more for off-Amazon AI visibility.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR] LLM Pulse studied 391,073 citations from non-branded U.S. shopping questions, collected 12 Apr – 11 Jul 2026.
  - Major retailers' share of citations: 6.05% for ChatGPT, 2.14% for Google AI Mode, 1.92% for AI Overviews, 0.92% for Gemini.
  - ChatGPT cited amazon.com only 6 times in the whole sample.
  - Amazon's average citation position was 9.67.

  — [LLM Pulse](https://llmpulse.ai/blog/where-ai-sends-shoppers/); [LLM Pulse ChatGPT data](https://llmpulse.ai/data-studies/top-us-retailers/chatgpt); [AI Overviews data](https://llmpulse.ai/data-studies/top-us-retailers/ai-overview)
- [VENDOR] BrightEdge (2025 holiday), via Search Engine Land: retailers made up about 4% of Google AI Overview citations but about 36% of ChatGPT's, with Amazon among ChatGPT's top sources. Google leaned towards user-generated content and expert reviews. — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-ai-vs-chatgpt-citations-retailers-465898); [BrightEdge](https://www.brightedge.com/resources/weekly-ai-search-insights/who-does-ai-trust-google-vs-chatgpt-citation-patterns)
- [PRESS] Amazon has blocked 47 AI bots from scraping its site. Jassy has said Amazon wants to partner with third-party agents eventually. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/rufus-shows-5-products-not-50-what-brands-must-know-about-amazons-ai-filter/)
- [PRESS] Related Oct 2026 context: Google launched Universal Cart for AI shopping, and eMarketer reports most big brands neglect robots.txt in their AI search strategies. I saw only the headlines and did not read these. — [eMarketer: Universal Cart](https://www.emarketer.com/content/google-universal-cart-ai-shopping-holiday-2026); [eMarketer: robots.txt](https://www.emarketer.com/content/most-big-brands-neglecting-robots-txt-files-part-of-their-ai-search-strategies)
- [AGENCY] A view that off-Amazon and on-Amazon AI run on "two stacks". — [paz.ai](https://www.paz.ai/blog/alexa-for-shopping-rufus-rename-two-stacks)

### Inferences
- For ChatGPT and Perplexity visibility, a brand's own site (crawlable product pages, clear specs, structured data) and third-party reviews and editorial coverage likely matter more than the Amazon listing. Google surfaces cite Amazon more.
- Amazon's Shop Direct and Buy for Me also index brand-site products. A clean D2C site therefore helps both off-Amazon AI and Amazon's own assistant.

### Gaps
- I found no credible data specific to Perplexity on Amazon citations.
- Neither study isolates product-detail-page citations, and the two studies conflict sharply on ChatGPT.
